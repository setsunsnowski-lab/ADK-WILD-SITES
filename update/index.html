    Adirondack Free Camps

    Designated free campsites, lean-tos and tent sites on New York State Forest Preserve land in the Adirondack Park, with GPS coordinates. No account, no ads, works without cell service.

    Reaching a site by car or motorcycle

    All sites are shown by default. In Filters, How you get there can narrow the map to sites judged drive-up: roadside sites and the Moose River Plains camping area. That is judged from each site’s name and area in the DEC file, not measured on a map, so it misses some roadside sites (they show as access not confirmed). Wilderness-type areas, where motor vehicles are not allowed, are marked hike or paddle in. Dirt roads can be rough for motorcycles and low cars, and many close for the season.

    Before you rely on a site

    • This list is incomplete and can be out of date. It comes from a NYS DEC export. Hikers report that official data misses many sites (some popular areas have far more than are listed), and closed sites can linger. Treat every pin as a lead to verify, not a guarantee.
    • Designated sites are marked on the ground, usually with a yellow “Camp Here” disc, and are first-come, first-served.
    • Groups of 10+ and stays of more than 3 nights in one place generally need a permit from the local DEC forest ranger. Bear canisters are required in the Eastern High Peaks Wilderness and a good idea everywhere.
    • Access roads are often closed seasonally, especially late fall to spring. Check conditions first.
    • Rules change and differ by area. Confirm on the DEC camping page before you go.

    Use it offline

    1. Open this page in Safari on Wi-Fi, tap Share, then Add to Home Screen. Launch it from the new icon.
    2. The campsite list, coordinates and distances are saved on your phone automatically.
    3. Map pictures are only saved for areas you have looked at, so before leaving, pan and zoom around your trip area (including the zoom levels you will want) while online.
    4. Your location uses the phone’s GPS and works with no signal. Distances are straight-line, not trail or road distance.

    About the data

    Source: New York State Department of Environmental Conservation (campsite and lean-to locations). The source has no fee field: sites are treated as free because they are designated sites on Forest Preserve land and DEC pay campgrounds were removed. Some areas may still require a permit. Sites in state forests and conservation easements, and rows with no listed land unit, are hidden by default. Map: © OpenStreetMap contributors; topographic style © OpenTopoMap (CC-BY-SA). Not an official DEC product.
